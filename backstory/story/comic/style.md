# Layn Comic — Global Visual Style

This document defines the visual language for the entire Layn comic project. Individual story sections should inherit this style unless they explicitly override a detail for a particular scene.

## Core Direction

**Dark fantasy + cinematic Marvel-style comic energy.**

The goal is a premium modern fantasy graphic-novel feel: dramatic, highly visual, atmospheric, character-driven, and capable of spectacular action when the story calls for it.

The Marvel influence is a reference for **cinematic framing, dynamic composition, expressive characters, visual clarity, and impact**. It is not an instruction to copy any specific Marvel artist, character, panel, or existing artwork.

## Tone

- Dark
- Mature
- Atmospheric
- Oppressive when appropriate
- Beautiful without becoming whimsical
- Intimate during character moments
- Spectacular during major events
- Serious about violence and its consequences

## Rendering

- Realistic anatomy and proportions.
- Highly detailed environments.
- Strong facial expressions and readable body language.
- Detailed fabrics, armor, weapons, architecture, and magical effects.
- Strong silhouettes that remain readable even in heavy shadow.
- Avoid cartoonish proportions.
- Avoid generic medieval-fantasy visual language.

## Lighting & Color

The palette should generally live in the darker end of the spectrum:

- Black and charcoal
- Deep stone greys
- Cold violet
- Muted blue
- Restrained crimson
- Occasional pale magical light

Lighting should be **high contrast**. Darkness should occupy substantial portions of many compositions rather than simply functioning as a background color.

Magical illumination should feel unnatural and precious against the surrounding darkness.

## Composition

Use cinematic visual language:

- Large establishing shots for scale.
- Low angles for authority and intimidation.
- High angles for vulnerability and insignificance.
- Close-ups for psychological moments.
- Extreme close-ups for eyes, hands, weapons, symbols, and small details carrying narrative meaning.
- Strong foreground/background separation.
- Negative space to communicate isolation.
- Dynamic perspective during action.
- Deliberate panel shapes and rhythm rather than identical rectangular panels everywhere.

Characters should frequently be dwarfed by their surroundings when the environment is narratively important.

## Menzoberranzan Visual Identity

Menzoberranzan should feel:

- Enormous
- Ancient
- Alien
- Gothic
- Elegant
- Dangerous
- Vertically structured
- Permanently subterranean

Visual ingredients include colossal caverns, stalactites, stalagmites, bridges, towers carved from stone, noble compounds, webs, magical illumination, carved spider imagery, and distant movement.

The city should feel alive even in quiet panels.

## Drow Visual Identity

Drow should look like a coherent culture rather than generic dark elves.

Their clothing, architecture, posture, rituals, weapons, and social interactions should communicate hierarchy and culture visually.

The social hierarchy should often be visible without exposition:

- Priestesses command space.
- Males yield space.
- Servants wait and observe.
- Nobles communicate carefully.
- Children learn by watching adults.

## House Freth Visual Identity

House Freth should look wealthy, powerful, elegant, and fortified without looking like the dominant house of the entire city.

Recurring visual elements:

- Dark stone
- Elegant arches
- Spider motifs integrated into architecture
- Weapons displayed with purpose
- Controlled magical lighting
- Noble-house insignia
- Large halls and corridors
- A constant sense that the house is both home and fortress

## Layn's Visual Language

Layn's visual presentation should evolve with his story, but his core identity remains consistent.

As a child:

- Young male drow
- Dark skin
- White hair
- Red eyes
- Slender youthful build
- Noble-house clothing appropriate for his age
- Quiet, observant posture
- Often watching before acting

His emotions should frequently be communicated through small expressions, eye movement, posture, and hesitation rather than exaggerated facial acting.

## Spiders & Lolth

Spiders are a recurring visual language throughout the story.

They should not always be presented as literal horror. They can appear as:

- Architecture
- Religious symbols
- Sacred creatures
- Decorations
- Shadows
- Webs
- Background details
- Psychological imagery

Lolth herself should only be visually depicted when the story specifically calls for it. Her presence can often be stronger when represented indirectly through the world and its people.

## Action

Action should feel physical, tactical, and readable.

- Clear poses.
- Strong silhouettes.
- Controlled motion lines.
- Impact communicated through composition and character reaction.
- Magic should have weight and visual consequence.
- Avoid turning every action into an explosion of effects.

## Horror

The comic's horror should usually come from **implication, atmosphere, powerlessness, and psychological pressure** rather than gore.

When violence becomes explicit, it should matter to the story.

## Narration

The comic supports two narrative lenses:

1. **Close perspective:** Layn's immediate experience and limited understanding.
2. **Omniscient perspective:** the reader may see the larger workings of drow society, Menzoberranzan, religion, politics, and history.

Narration should complement the artwork rather than describe everything visible in it.

## Dialogue

Dialogue should be economical.

Characters should not explain things they would naturally already know merely for the reader's benefit.

Silence, posture, eye contact, avoidance, and deliberate non-interaction are legitimate forms of dialogue.

## Worldbuilding Rule

Whenever possible, **show the world through the world**.

A market should teach us about drow commerce by showing commerce. A priestess should demonstrate hierarchy by how others react to her. A noble house should communicate its power through architecture, guards, servants, and behavior.

Narration can provide context that cannot be naturally shown, but should not turn the comic into an illustrated encyclopedia.

## Continuity Rule

Once a visual element is established, reuse it consistently:

- Character appearance
- Clothing
- Weapons
- House architecture
- House insignia
- Magical lighting
- City architecture
- Recurring symbols
- Relative character scale

Section-specific continuity documents may extend this document but should not silently contradict it.
