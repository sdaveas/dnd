Chapter 11 — Conspiracy

“Psionic?” asked Dreydre.

“Possibly,” said Greyanna.

The Matron Mother stood before them in perfect composure, though only a few weeks had passed since she had given birth to the youngest male of House Freth.

Laynlindr Freth was still too young to speak. He slept in the chamber beyond the inner doors, watched by servants who had been instructed to report anything unusual.

Dreydre was the first to speak again.

“If it is true, he could become dangerous.”

“Or uncontrollable,” Greyanna added with equal concern.

The Matron Mother listened without interrupting.

Neither sister spoke for a moment.

“Then we should kill him now and avoid all risk,” Dreydre said.

Greyanna looked at her sister, then toward the doors again. “Before he reaches.”

The Matron Mother turned her gaze upon them.

“In this child lies the work of Lolth.”

The sisters fell silent.

“If she has placed such power within the boy, then we will not destroy it before understanding why. We will not act in haste and risk bringing Lolth's displeasure upon us.”

She paused, allowing her words to sink in.

“We will welcome her gift. It is now our task to determine how we are to use it.”

Dreydre's eyes sparked.

“Otherwise, we risk bringing her rage upon us by interfering in her divine work,” Dreydre said.

The Matron Mother gave her a brief look.

“You should have understood that by now.”

A figure appeared in the doorway.

Zilvrae entered, bowed once, and waited.

“You have summoned me, Matron Mother.”

Her eyes moved briefly between the Matron Mother and the two daughters. The sisters' expressions were more annoyed than usual.

“We require your knowledge. What we are dealing with is within your domain, after all.”

The final words carried just enough weight to make the remark something other than courtesy.

Zilvrae did not react.

“Training?”

“Younglings,” the Matron Mother said.

The Matron Mother's gaze remained on her.

“There are ambiguous signs that the youngest male may be psionic.”

Zilvrae thought for a second.

“I can recognize psionic manifestations, but I cannot look at an infant and tell you what his mind may become. What you seek requires a psionic scholar's expertise.”

All that was already clear to the Matron Mother.

“Then tell me this,” said the Matron Mother. “How would you determine whether the power is there? In your experience, you should have seen many children manifest their gifts.”

Zilvrae considered the question.

“I have never had a psionic student. But assuming my understanding of psionic powers is even remotely correct, then there is a direct way, and there is a safe way,” she said. “However, these two do not align.”

Greyanna folded her arms.

The Matron Mother waited.

“The direct way,” Zilvrae said curtly, “is to attack him.”

The sisters stared at her.

“Powerfully enough to cause permanent damage, or destroy him entirely. Enough to force his instincts to answer before his mind understands what is happening. At his age, instinct governs almost everything.”

Zilvrae's voice remained level.

“If the power is present, he may manifest some form of barrier before his conscious mind even knows that it has done so. If the reaction is strong enough, the pressure may continue until the power breaks outward.”

She looked around the chamber.

“I would not recommend doing it in this gallery,” she added calmly. “If the manifestation is strong enough, you may have to rebuild it afterward.”

“The safest,” Zilvrae continued, “would be House Oblodra.”

Silence followed.

Dreydre was the first to speak.

“You expect us to hand them a secret weapon?”

“No.”

Zilvrae looked at her directly.

“But I expect you to understand that they possess knowledge I do not.”

“And they would learn what House Freth possesses.”

“Unacceptable,” the Matron Mother whispered to herself.

“Of course,” said Zilvrae without arguing.

House Oblodra was among the most powerful Houses in Menzoberranzan, and its members possessed knowledge of the rare art of psionics. There was little doubt that they would be able to identify the boy's nature, its potential, perhaps even the limits of what he might become. But no drow House would open its secrets that way without revealing something valuable in return.

“Then there is the middle ground,” Zilvrae said.

All eyes in the room focused on her.

“Calmness.”

Greyanna's mouth tightened.

“As you all know, psionic power is a power of the mind,” Zilvrae said. “The mind of an infant has barely begun to distinguish itself from instinct. There is little to learn by forcing it.”

“Then what do you propose?” asked the Matron Mother.

“Patience. If the power is there, it will emerge organically. To my knowledge, it is not uncommon even for children who show signs of such powers to lose them by the time they begin to understand the world around them. Being born with the power is one thing. Retaining it is another. And I am not familiar with methods to steer the child toward one outcome or the other.”

The answer seemed almost deliberately simple.

Zilvrae continued before anyone could object.

“As he grows, he will begin to develop a will of his own. He will want things. Seek things. Refuse things. That is when the power, if it exists, will begin to show, shaped by the character of its wielder.”

The Matron Mother watched her closely.

Zilvrae looked toward the inner chamber.

The Matron Mother was silent for a long moment.

Then she turned to her daughters.

“You will be responsible for his training when he is old enough.”

Zilvrae inclined her head.

“You will not encourage him to look inward for answers. You will not speak to him of the power we suspect he possesses. You will not help him seek it.”

Even though Zilvrae's expression barely changed.

“Understood,” she said.

“Now, leave us.”

The tutor bowed, turned, and closed the doors to the Matron Mother's chambers behind her.

The Matron Mother's command had struck her. How far did the potential of that boy reach, and how much of it would be limited by this conspiracy? She had always despised the way the members of noble Houses treated their children. Nevertheless, that was the state of things in Menzoberranzan. She would oblige the Matron Mother's orders, but she would use every possible opportunity to instill her own beliefs in the young boy. In the experienced tutor's mind, some lessons were more useful than knowing how to wield blades. Lessons that no drow—least of all a female—would dare convey.

---

“You will watch him,” the Matron Mother said, turning to the sisters after Zilvrae left.

Dreydre's expression hardened.

“From a distance,” the Matron Mother continued. “You will observe. You will not provoke him. You will not frighten him. You will not hurt him merely to produce evidence.”

Greyanna's jaw tightened as though the Matron Mother's words had slapped her.

“If he reaches,” the Matron Mother continued, “you will suppress the manifestation.”

“Yes, Matron Mother,” the sisters said simultaneously.
