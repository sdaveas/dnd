# Layn — Main Story Decisions

This file records the main-scenario decisions established while refining Layn's backstory. It deliberately avoids implementation details that will be decided during the actual prose and encounter-design phases.

## Core identity

- Layn is Laynlindr Freth, a male drow.
- He considers himself a drow, but one who walks his own path.
- He retains the language, magic, knowledge, and weapon training he learned in Menzoberranzan.
- He despises drow society and what Lolth represents. His rejection is of the way of life, not a denial that he himself is drow.

## Before the House Freth attack

- Layn already has strong negative feelings toward drow society before the attack.
- He has never seriously planned to leave the Underdark, however. Leaving happens organically during the House Freth attack.
- His family is not uniquely responsible for his hatred of drow society. They are simply his closest and earliest example of what being drow means to him.
- Layn despises his family, but that feeling is part of his broader rejection of drow society.

## The House Freth attack

- The attack exposes Layn to a level of war, violence, brutality, and treachery he has never witnessed before.
- He is initially stunned by the violence.
- In the chaos, he realizes that he wants nothing to do with this society regardless of which side wins.
- This realization is the final nail in the coffin of his already-existing rejection of drow society.
- His conclusion is essentially: **he is out**.
- He does not make a calculated political choice between the attacking house and House Freth.

## Layn's role in the climactic battle

- Layn is present during the climactic confrontation involving the Matron Mother of House Freth and two high priestesses from the opposing house.
- He is a level-1 character at this point, but has been trained as a drow soldier.
- He has a legitimate opportunity to influence the battle.
- He could potentially interfere with either side, including by exploiting a concentration-disruption opportunity. Exact mechanics are intentionally deferred.
- He refuses to intervene because he wants nothing to do with drow or their ways.
- His defining thought is not indecision or fear. It is simply: **I'm out.**
- He does not help the Matron Mother.
- He does not help the opposing priestesses.
- He does not directly kill any of them.
- The exact D&D encounter, spells, levels, positions, concentration checks, and final clash will be designed later.
- The required story outcome remains that the three priestesses ultimately die in the resulting confrontation, including the Matron Mother of House Freth.

## The escape

- Layn uses the havoc of the battle to disappear.
- His survival should foreshadow the rogue he will eventually become: his instinct is to hide, remain unseen, and exploit the chaos rather than fight openly.
- The exact hiding place and exact method by which his death is assumed are deliberately left for later.
- He successfully disappears and is believed to have died in the destruction.
- He travels alone.
- There is no established friend, companion, or escape partner.
- No invented characters are to be added to this part of the story.

## Why he goes toward the surface

- Layn initially intends only to get away from Menzoberranzan and drow society; he does not originally formulate a plan to live on the surface.
- His original inclination after leaving is simply to remain somewhere in the Underdark, away from drow society.
- The surface becomes part of his story because **Lolth makes it so**.

## Lolth

- Before the House Freth conflict, Lolth was barely aware of Layn.
- There was no prior personal relationship or longstanding divine conflict between them.
- Layn becomes personally relevant to Lolth only after his refusal to participate in the House Freth conflict.
- Layn hates Lolth because he hates everything she represents. To him, Lolth the deity and Lolth's methods are inseparable.
- Lolth hates Layn in return.
- Her response is deliberately both punishment and blessing, with the two intertwined.

## The cocoon and transformation

- After Layn has escaped into the Underdark, Lolth's servants find him.
- They capture and torture him.
- He is immobilized and placed inside a spider-like cocoon.
- He remains trapped for months while his body is transformed.
- His skin becomes pale/white, making him appear to be a surface elf.
- Layn knows that Lolth captured him, tortured him, and transformed him.
- He interprets the pale appearance as Lolth's punishment.
- Lolth, however, deliberately understands that the pale appearance will also function as a tool allowing Layn to interact with surface people without immediately being treated as a drow.
- Lolth lets him live.
- Lolth also plants or awakens the idea of embracing the surface through a vision. The exact content of that vision is intentionally left for the writing phase.
- When Layn wakes from the cocoon, he does not understand exactly where he is, but mysteriously understands that he is close to a tunnel leading toward the surface.

## The hidden nature of the blessing

The reader should understand the twisted nature of Lolth's blessing even if Layn does not understand the full plan.

Lolth's intention is not merely to punish Layn immediately. She wants him to use the pale form as a means to enter surface society, build friendships and relationships, become accepted, and eventually feel that he belongs there.

Only after Layn has built something meaningful does the second half of the punishment take effect: the pale form will eventually disappear, leaving him exposed as a drow and threatening the life and relationships he has built.

Thus:

- the curse contains the means of survival;
- the blessing is also the mechanism of the eventual torment;
- the physical punishment is only the beginning;
- the greater punishment is psychological.

The exact identity and dialogue of the servants who reveal this to the reader are intentionally left for the actual writing phase. They know the plan.

## What the transformation is

The precise mechanism remains intentionally ambiguous.

Possible interpretations retained for future development:

1. A non-magical fundamental transformation that the body eventually reverses.
2. A divine curse whose magic has become so deeply incorporated into Layn's essence that it is no longer externally detectable as an active magical effect.

Layn can detect magic as a drow, but he detects nothing magical about the transformation itself. The effect is treated as intrinsic to him rather than as an obvious spell that could simply be identified or dispelled.

We should not resolve this ambiguity prematurely.

## The next story

The eventual surface life is not part of this backstory.

The next story will deal with Layn discovering who he is and what his identity means. The eventual reversion from pale elf to drow belongs to that later story.

## Ending

The backstory ends when Layn reaches the end of the Underdark tunnel and sees the surface light leading toward Luskan.

The emotional tone of this ending is important:

- hope;
- relief;
- anticipation;
- the feeling of a new beginning;
- some reasonable stress and anxiety because the future is unknown.

Layn does **not** regret leaving.

The main story should allow the reader to experience this as a hopeful ending for a character who has suffered greatly.

After the apparent ending, a separate post-ending scene may reveal the conversation of Lolth's servants and expose the reader to the true plan: Layn's pale skin will eventually wear away after he has become comfortable and rebuilt his life on the surface.

The post-ending scene should preserve the dramatic irony: **the reader can celebrate Layn's new beginning while knowing that Lolth has designed that new beginning as part of his eventual torment.**
