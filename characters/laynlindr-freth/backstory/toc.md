# Layn — Backstory Table of Contents

This directory contains the prose version of Layn's backstory, developed separately from [`notes.md`](notes.md).

`notes.md` remains the source of truth for story decisions, canon constraints, unresolved details, and planning.

The [character cheatsheet](cast.md) is the continuity reference for names, relationships, motives, voices, and status labels across the novel, audio, and comic adaptations.

The backstory is organized by **character → backstory → chapter**. Each chapter is a standalone prose file; audio renders and comic scripts remain in their respective production directories. Individual titles and scene boundaries may evolve as the prose is written.

## Story Structure

The backstory has two parts. **Part One** covers Layn's life in House Freth up to the Hearing in his twentieth year. **Part Two** covers the Academy, his years back in House Freth, the Teken'duis attack, his flight through the Underdark, and ends when he steps onto the surface. Dates and the setup-and-payoff ledger are in [`notes.md` §24](notes.md#24-part-one-revision-decisions).

## Narrative Approach

The story combines two lenses:

- **Layn's perspective:** an intimate view of his life and development as a young drow. His understanding is limited by his age and circumstances; he should not think with the knowledge of an outside observer.
- **Omniscient perspective:** a wider view of Menzoberranzan, drow society, politics, religion, customs, and history, including information Layn does not yet understand.

A complete subsection may follow another character—especially one of Layn's older sisters—when that viewpoint reveals motives or actions Layn cannot see. Viewpoint changes should happen at subsection boundaries rather than through abrupt head-hopping.

Worldbuilding should be embedded in lived scenes wherever possible rather than presented as encyclopedia-style exposition.

The prose should have its own voice. *Homeland* is a reference for immersive worldbuilding and narrative architecture, not a template for copying R. A. Salvatore's prose style.

The final prose should read naturally aloud: varied sentence length, clear paragraph rhythm, strong imagery, dialogue that is easy to follow when spoken, and enough breathing room for atmosphere and emotional beats.
