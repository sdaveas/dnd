# Layn — Backstory Notes

> Working notes for the original Layn storyline. This is a homebrew narrative layer intended to fit around the established Forgotten Realms / Menzoberranzan setting and the published Salvatore material. It is **not** presented as official canon.
>
> Canon is the fixed skeleton of the story. Layn's personal history is the additional layer placed inside it. If a published source establishes an event or outcome, our story must work around it rather than overwrite it.

## 1. Narrative approach

- The story is our own additional layer inside the existing Forgotten Realms setting.
- Published events, characters, chronology, and outcomes are treated as fixed unless a source leaves something unspecified.
- The published story provides the canonical framework; our story adds Layn's personal puzzle piece inside the gaps.
- If our planned story conflicts with established canon, canon wins and Layn's story must be adjusted.
- The precise reasons for conflicts between houses should not be invented unless supported by canon or explicitly decided later.
- Where the published sources do not establish a detail, that detail is **OUR STORY** and should be treated as such.

## 2. Layn's family and childhood

- Layn is **Laynlindr Freth**, a male drow of House Freth.
- Layn's childhood was difficult and abusive within his family.
- His sisters tormented him deliberately and cruelly.
- One particularly important form of torment involved the sisters summoning or obtaining **unnaturally large spiders** and placing them in Layn's bed.
- They told young Layn that killing the spiders, driving them away, or otherwise interfering with them would be **blasphemy against Lolth**.
- Layn therefore believed he had no choice but to lie still while the spiders crawled across his skin.
- This became a profound psychological trauma that Layn still carries into adulthood.
- Even years later, seeing a spider can trigger the memory and fear associated with what his sisters did to him.
- His mother, the Matron Mother of House Freth, **ignored him altogether**. She never interacted with him and never even met his eyes.

## 3. Why Layn was sent to the warriors' school

Layn's path toward the warriors' school was driven by his **sisters**.

The sisters had two connected reasons for wanting Layn directed toward the warriors' school rather than the mage school:

- They were jealous and afraid of Layn's potential. They sensed that he might eventually become unusually powerful and possibly surpass them.
- They had also sensed signs of Layn's **psionic potential**. The possibility of a male drow developing significant psionic abilities frightened them.

They did not want to risk Layn developing those powers through the mage path and potentially becoming powerful enough to outshine them — particularly because Lolth's priestesses were the goddess's female representatives and wielded religious and political authority in drow society.

Therefore, the warriors' school was the safer path from their perspective. It gave Layn a controlled role while reducing the perceived risk that he would develop into a male drow whose extraordinary abilities could rival or overshadow Lolth's representatives.

This is an intentional part of Layn's original story.

## 4. Layn and Lolth before the House Freth attack

Lolth was **barely aware that Layn existed**.

His sisters had kept him suppressed and insignificant, including his psionic potential. To Lolth, he was otherwise just another unremarkable male drow — nothing that demanded her attention.

There was therefore **no prior relationship between Layn and Lolth** and no longstanding divine conflict between them.

That changes only later, when Layn's refusal to act during the House Freth conflict finally brings him to Lolth's attention.

## 5. The House Freth attack — canon first

The relevant pages of *Homeland* now available to us establish the canonical framework for the attack. The exact placement of Layn inside these events remains our story and must not overwrite the published outcome.

- House Freth is attacked by **House Teken'Duis**.
- The attack devastates House Freth.
- The published aftermath explicitly accounts for **three surviving young members of House Freth: two females and one male**.
- Those surviving members are taken under the protection of **House Baenre**.
- The attacking House Teken'Duis is subsequently targeted by Menzoberranzan's retaliation and is destroyed.
- **Layn is not one of the three canonical survivors.** The three survivors in the published scene are other members of House Freth.
- Layn instead survives the attack and **fakes his own death**, allowing him to disappear without changing the canonical group of survivors.
- Layn's exact position and actions during the attack are our story and must be fitted around the published sequence.

## 6. The decisive moment in Layn's version of the battle

At the apex of the attack, Layn reaches the halls of the Matron Mother of House Freth.

The Matron Mother of House Freth — **Martin Freth** in our current story terminology — is engaged in a desperate confrontation with **two powerful high priestesses from the opposing house**.

The eventual combat will be designed later as an actual D&D encounter. For the story outline, the only required facts are:

- The Matron Mother is vastly more powerful than Layn.
- The two opposing priestesses are also vastly more powerful than Layn.
- Layn nevertheless has a legitimate opportunity to affect the fight.
- He is a trained drow soldier by this point, so he can plausibly exploit a brief tactical opening even as a level-1 character.
- For example, he may have an opportunity to attack one priestess and potentially disrupt her concentration, or use another tactical option available to a trained drow warrior.
- The exact spells, levels, battlefield, mechanics, and final magical clash are intentionally **left for later**.

The important narrative point is that Layn understands that he can influence the outcome.

## 7. Layn's three possible choices

At the decisive moment, Layn can choose among three paths.

### 1. Break an opposing priestess's concentration

Layn can intervene against one of the attacking priestesses and help his own house.

This would help House Freth, oppose the attacking side, and potentially advance Lolth's plan for that moment.

This is the most straightforwardly drow response: recognize the opportunity, choose the side that benefits you, and act decisively.

### 2. Break the Matron Mother's concentration

Layn can instead intervene against the Matron Mother and help bring about the fall of his own house.

This would undermine House Freth, assist the attacking side, and potentially advance Lolth's plan if Lolth desires the fall of House Freth.

This is the twisted, two-faced, treacherous option. It is the kind of opportunism that can flourish within drow society: betray your own house when circumstances make that betrayal advantageous.

### 3. Do nothing

Layn can simply refuse to intervene.

This is fundamentally different from the first two choices.

A drow is expected to recognize opportunities for advantage and act according to circumstance. Both active choices are self-interested and recognizably drow responses.

Layn instead chooses **inaction**.

He does not save the Matron Mother.
He does not attack the opposing priestesses.
He does not deliberately choose either side's victory.

His only direct influence on the battle is his refusal to act when he could have acted.

## 8. The outcome of the priestess confrontation

The exact combat will be balanced and resolved later using actual D&D mechanics.

For the story outline, the required outcome is:

- Layn does nothing.
- The magical confrontation continues without his intervention.
- The three priestesses ultimately die in the resulting clash.
- **Martin Freth, the Matron Mother of House Freth, dies in the attack.**
- Layn does not directly kill her and does not directly kill either opposing priestess.

The exact mechanism — including spells, concentration checks, environmental effects, and the final clash of forces — remains deliberately unresolved until we design the encounter.

## 9. Why Layn's inaction is the paradox

The paradox is that **Layn follows a non-drow / non-Lolth path, and that refusal can nevertheless end up benefiting Lolth**.

The first two choices are opposite actions, but both follow the underlying logic of drow society:

- be faithful when faithfulness benefits you;
- betray when betrayal benefits you;
- seize the opportunity when power or circumstance gives you an advantage;
- act according to self-interest and the immediate balance of power.

Layn's third choice is unnatural by comparison. He does not seize the opportunity. He simply refuses to choose.

And yet his refusal can accidentally produce an outcome favorable to Lolth.

That is what makes the situation offensive to Lolth: Layn has achieved something that resembles obedience without actually obeying her.

The same decision can therefore be both a rejection of Lolth's way and an accidental benefit to Lolth's plans.

Lolth's response should preserve that contradiction rather than resolve it.

## 10. Layn fakes his death and flees

After the House Freth battle, Layn deliberately makes it appear that he died in the destruction of the house.

- The canonical three surviving Freth members remain the other members described in *Homeland*.
- Layn is not added to or substituted into that canonical group.
- Layn's apparent death allows him to disappear from the aftermath without altering the published sequence.
- He follows a pre-planned escape route.
- No friend, companion, or escape partner has been established for this part of the story.
- No character named Zacharios, Clynto, Kleinto, or Glinz is part of the confirmed backstory.
- Layn travels alone through the Underdark toward the surface.

## 11. Lolth finally notices Layn

Only after the House Freth conflict does Lolth truly take notice of Layn.

She sees that an otherwise insignificant male drow deliberately refused to take either of the active paths available to him. His inaction is not a normal act of drow opportunism, and yet it may have produced an outcome favorable to her.

This is the moment when Layn becomes personally relevant to Lolth.

Her response is deliberately contradictory: part punishment, part blessing.

## 12. Lolth's punishment and blessing

### The curse

**1. Exile**

Lolth ensures that Layn's separation from Menzoberranzan becomes more than a voluntary escape. Even if Layn chose to leave willingly, returning home is no longer something he can simply reclaim without consequence.

**2. Transformation into an elf**

Lolth subjects Layn to prolonged physical torture by transforming his body into that of a pale/white-skinned surface elf.

The transformation is painful and humiliating. Layn remains a drow in identity, history, instincts, and inner nature; only his physical form has been monstrously altered.

For a long period, this makes returning to his homeland effectively impossible. His altered appearance makes him look like the kind of surface elf that drow society regards as an enemy.

### The blessing

**1. Lolth does not kill him.**

Despite her anger, Lolth allows Layn to survive. His survival is itself a form of twisted mercy.

**2. Lolth gives him the means to survive in the upper world.**

His pale/white appearance allows him to pass among surface peoples in a way that his true drow appearance would not. The very transformation that exiles him therefore gives him the tool he needs to survive his exile.

The punishment contains the means of survival, while the gift contains the punishment.

## 13. The spider cocoon punishment

Lolth's punishment is deliberately elaborate and cruel.

- Several days after the House Freth event, Layn is away from Menzoberranzan, traveling through the Underdark toward the surface.
- Strange spider-like beings or manifestations associated with Lolth attack him.
- They immobilize him and enclose him in a large cocoon resembling that of a spider or transforming creature.
- Only enough of his face / nose remains exposed for him to breathe.
- The cocoon keeps him alive while forcing him through prolonged suffering.
- Layn remains trapped for **months**.
- The experience is intensely painful and transformative.

The spider imagery is especially cruel because of Layn's childhood trauma: the same creatures his sisters used to torment him now become the instrument of Lolth's punishment.

The transformation should be described using magical language rather than modern biological terminology.

## 14. Layn's transformation

Over the months in the cocoon, Lolth's magic alters Layn's body.

- His physical form changes substantially.
- His skin eventually becomes pale/white rather than the normal dark drow coloration.
- He comes to appear outwardly like a surface elf rather than a drow.
- Internally and spiritually, however, he remains fundamentally himself: a drow carrying his origin, memories, instincts, and history.
- The transformation is not a simple illusion or disguise. It is a genuine magical alteration of his body.
- The physical pain of the transformation is part of the punishment.

The symbolism is deliberate: Lolth takes the thing that marks Layn as a drow and changes it into the appearance of the people his homeland regards as enemies.

## 15. The cruel survival mechanism

The transformation simultaneously solves and creates Layn's next problem.

Layn is now effectively cut off from Menzoberranzan and the normal drow world.

- A visibly drow Layn would attract fear, suspicion, and potentially violence on the surface.
- His pale appearance gives him a chance to pass as an ordinary elf.
- Therefore, the curse itself becomes the means by which he can survive in the upper world.

Lolth has not simply banished Layn. She has constructed a trap in which **survival requires him to live as someone he is not**.

## 16. The hidden final twist: the magic will shatter

There is a further layer to Lolth's punishment that **Layn does not know**.

The transformation is sustained by Lolth's divine magic. In our story, that magic is strongly tied to the Underdark and darkness and becomes unstable when Layn is removed from that environment — especially when he is repeatedly exposed to the light of the sun.

The intended consequence is:

- the longer Layn lives on the surface, the more the divine magic sustaining his altered appearance is weakened;
- sunlight accelerates or intensifies that weakening;
- eventually the transformation will **shatter**;
- Layn will return to his true dark-elf appearance.

This is **OUR STORY / homebrew lore** until we establish a Forgotten Realms-compatible mechanism that does not contradict published material.

Crucially, **Layn does not know this will happen**.

He believes the transformation is his new reality. He will only understand its impermanence later, when the magic begins to fail.

## 17. Why Lolth waits

The timing of the curse is part of its cruelty.

Lolth does not merely want Layn to suffer immediately. She wants him to build something first.

Layn will spend significant time among surface peoples. He will:

- learn how to live above ground;
- make friends;
- form relationships;
- become accepted;
- begin to heal from the life he had in Menzoberranzan;
- develop an identity that is no longer entirely defined by House Freth and Lolth.

Only **after** he has something to lose will the curse reveal its final consequence.

The transformation's failure therefore becomes a delayed weapon.

## 18. Future reversal / reveal

At some future point, potentially during a major event in the Underdark, the magic sustaining Layn's transformed appearance will fail or be disrupted.

When that happens:

- Layn's true drow appearance returns.
- His dark skin becomes visible again.
- People who knew him as a surface elf realize that he was actually a drow.
- Relationships built during his years on the surface are suddenly placed under extreme pressure.
- Layn himself is forced to confront the possibility that Lolth's punishment was designed to make him build attachments before taking them away.

This is future campaign material, not part of the backstory itself.

## 19. The ending of the backstory

**The backstory ends when Layn reaches the end of the Underdark tunnel.**

After everything that has happened, he finally sees light ahead.

It is not the glow of Underdark fungi or magical illumination. It is the light of the surface.

Beyond the tunnel lies the world above — and, specifically, **Luskan**.

Layn does not yet know what awaits him there. He does not know how he will survive, who he will meet, or what kind of life he will eventually build.

The backstory ends at that threshold, with Layn looking toward the light leading out of the Underdark to Luskan.

The later years on the surface, his relationships, and the eventual failure of Lolth's transformation belong to the campaign's future rather than to this origin story.

## 20. Themes

The Layn storyline is built around several connected themes:

- **Agency vs. divine control:** Layn's defining decision is his refusal to choose the battle's outcome for Lolth.
- **The drow instinct for opportunism:** both active choices available to Layn embody the willingness to exploit circumstances for advantage.
- **The unnatural third choice:** Layn's refusal to act is what separates him from the expected drow response.
- **Lolth's paradox:** the same decision can accidentally benefit Lolth while simultaneously denying her direct control.
- **Cruel mercy:** Lolth does not kill Layn; instead, she preserves him for a more elaborate punishment.
- **Punishment as a gift:** the transformation is torture and exile, but also the tool that allows Layn to survive above ground.
- **Identity:** Layn looks like a surface elf while remaining a drow in origin and experience.
- **Exile:** he is cut off from Menzoberranzan and from the identity he once inhabited.
- **Delayed cruelty:** Lolth allows Layn to build a life before the transformation ultimately fails.
- **The hidden self:** Layn's true identity remains concealed beneath his altered appearance.
- **The spider:** the creature used to torment him as a child becomes a recurring symbol of Lolth's control and cruelty.

## 22. Canon verification rule

Before treating any setting detail as established Forgotten Realms canon, verify it against the relevant published source.

Use three labels in future notes:

- **CANON** — directly supported by a cited published source.
- **COMPATIBLE / INFERRED** — not explicitly stated, but designed to fit the published setting without contradiction.
- **OUR STORY** — an original Layn-specific addition created for this campaign.

The goal is not to replace the published story. The goal is to place Layn's story inside the spaces where the published narrative does not specify every individual person's actions.
